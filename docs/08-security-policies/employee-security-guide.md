# Employee Security Guide

Plain security advice for everyone, with no jargon. This is the one page every employee should read: what to do, what to avoid, and how to get help. Share it on day one and keep it short enough that people actually read it.

!!! abstract "At a glance"
    - Audience: every employee, no technical background assumed
    - Time to read: 6 minutes
    - Outcome: the handful of habits that stop most attacks

## The short version

If you only remember five things:

- [ ] **Think before you click.** Unexpected links and attachments are how most attacks start.
- [ ] **Use strong, unique passwords and a password manager.** Never reuse your work password anywhere.
- [ ] **Turn on MFA** (the app or key that confirms it is really you) everywhere you can.
- [ ] **Report anything odd, straight away.** Better a false alarm than a silent breach.
- [ ] **If you think you made a mistake, say so immediately.** You will not be blamed; speed is everything.

## Email and messages

Most attacks arrive as a message pretending to be someone you trust.

- Be wary of **urgency and pressure**: "do this now", "your account will be closed", "the CEO needs this payment today". Urgency is a trick.
- Check the **sender's real address**, not just the display name. Attackers fake names easily.
- **Hover over links** to see where they really go before clicking. On a phone, press and hold.
- Do not open **unexpected attachments**, especially ones that ask you to enable editing or content.
- If an email asks you to **log in**, go to the site yourself in the browser instead of clicking the link.
- A message about **money or bank details** that arrives by email should be confirmed by phone to a known number, never a number from the email.

!!! tip "When in doubt, slow down and check"
    Attackers rely on you being busy and reacting fast. A ten-second pause to check the sender, hover over the link, or phone the person is the single most effective thing you can do. It is never rude to verify.

## Passwords and accounts

- Use a **different password for every account**. A password manager remembers them so you do not have to.
- Make work passwords **long**. A memorable phrase of several words beats a short one with symbols.
- **Never share your password**, not with IT, not with your manager, not with a caller. Real IT will never ask for it.
- Turn on **MFA** wherever it is offered. If you get an MFA prompt you did not start, **deny it and report it**: someone may have your password.

## Your devices

- **Lock your screen** when you walk away (a quick key combination).
- Keep your devices **up to date**; install updates when asked.
- Do not install software or plug in USB sticks you are not sure about.
- Do not disable the **security software** on your work device.
- If a device is **lost or stolen, report it at once** so it can be locked.

## Working with data

- Keep company data in **approved company systems**, not personal email or personal cloud storage.
- Double-check the **recipient** before sending anything sensitive.
- Be careful what you **share and where**; a link set to "anyone can view" can go further than you think.
- At home or in public, be aware of **who can see your screen** and do not use untrusted public computers for work.

## Phone calls and in person

- Attackers also use **phone calls** ("this is IT, I need your password to fix something") and **fake urgency** from people pretending to be executives. Verify through a known channel.
- Do not let people you do not know **follow you into secure areas** (tailgating).
- Be careful what you share on **social media** about your work; attackers use it to craft convincing messages.

## If something goes wrong

- **Report it immediately** through `[the reporting method: email / button / service desk number]`.
- If you **clicked something, entered a password, or paid a suspicious invoice**, report it right away. The faster you report, the more the security team can do.
- **You will not be blamed** for an honest mistake or a false alarm. Reporting helps everyone.
- **Do not try to fix it yourself** by deleting things; keep the evidence and let the security team handle it.

!!! danger "The mistake is not clicking the link, it is staying silent"
    Anyone can be fooled by a good fake, including security experts. What turns a small mistake into a disaster is not reporting it. If you think you slipped up, tell the security team now, not tomorrow. They would far rather hear about it early.
