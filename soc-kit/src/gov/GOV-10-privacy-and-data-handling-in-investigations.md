## 1. Purpose

The SOC can see a great deal of personal data: who logged in from where, what they emailed, what they browsed. This policy sets how [ORGANISATION]'s SOC handles that data lawfully, proportionately and only for security, so investigations are both effective and defensible.

> WARNING: This is a legal and privacy matter that varies by country and may involve works councils and data-protection law. Have [legal and the Data Protection Officer] review and approve this policy. The guidance here is a starting point, not legal advice.

## 2. Principles

- [ ] **Purpose.** SOC access to personal data is for security monitoring and investigation only, not for tracking productivity or for other purposes.
- [ ] **Proportionality.** Collect and look at the least personal data needed to do the security job.
- [ ] **Lawfulness.** Monitoring has a lawful basis and is covered by a privacy notice that tells staff what is monitored and why.
- [ ] **Minimisation.** Do not copy or keep personal data beyond what the investigation needs.
- [ ] **Accountability.** Access to personal data is logged and reviewable.

## 3. Day-to-day investigations

- [ ] Analysts access logs and data for a security purpose tied to an alert or incident, not out of curiosity.
- [ ] Access to personal data is itself logged.
- [ ] Sensitive content (the body of emails, file contents, browsing) is looked at only when the investigation genuinely needs it, not by default.

## 4. Investigating a named person

Investigating a specific employee (for example a suspected insider) is different and needs extra authority.

- [ ] Do not begin targeted monitoring of a named person without written authorisation from [HR and legal], recorded on the HR Investigation Authorisation form.
- [ ] Scope the authorisation: which person, which systems, which time window.
- [ ] Restrict the case to named investigators.
- [ ] Do not let analysts interview staff; they collect technical facts only.

See the Insider Threat playbook and the HR Investigation Authorisation template.

## 5. Handling and storing personal data in a case

- [ ] Store case data, including any personal data, with access limited to the investigation team.
- [ ] Apply a legal hold where an incident may lead to legal action, suspending normal deletion of relevant data.
- [ ] Keep evidence per the Evidence Handling SOP, with chain of custody where it may be needed later.
- [ ] Delete or minimise personal data that is no longer needed, in line with retention rules.

## 6. Sharing

- [ ] Share personal data outside the investigation team only where lawful and necessary (for example with legal, HR, or a regulator as legal decides).
- [ ] Strip or mask personal data when sharing indicators externally (for example with an ISAC).

## 7. Cross-border and third parties

Where data crosses borders or a managed provider sees it, confirm the arrangement is lawful and covered by the relevant agreements, with [legal].
